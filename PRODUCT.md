# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two equally weighted audiences: potential **donors** who need to feel trust and impact before giving, and **community members** (people affected by allergies, caregivers, advocates) who come for awareness, events, and connection. Both arrive at the landing page; both must find a clear, relevant path within the first scroll.

## Product Purpose

PRSM Allergy Foundation is a nonprofit supporting allergy and immunology research, patient education, and community programs. The site's job is to convert visitors into donors and engaged community members: fundraising pages, event sign-ups, and newsletter subscriptions are the primary conversion surfaces. 100% of donations go directly to external allergy research organizations.

## Brand Personality

Warm, hopeful, and grounded. Three words: **human, purposeful, trustworthy**.

Tone is optimistic but not saccharine. Speaks like a knowledgeable friend who cares deeply about the cause, not a corporate press release or a medical textbook. The research context adds credibility; the community context adds warmth.

## Anti-references

- **Generic charity template**: no predictable three-column feature grid, no large orange CTAs on white, no stock-photo smiling-doctors aesthetic, no bold "DONATE NOW" hero that reads like a billboard.
- Avoid cold clinical aesthetics: white-on-white with blue accents, hospital-grade sterility.
- Avoid nonprofit cliche: the hero-metric template (giant number + label + gradient accent), identical icon cards.

## Design Principles

1. **Research gives us credibility; community gives us heart.** Let both show without flattening either into the other.
2. **Every page has one primary action.** Don't compete with equal-weight CTAs. Guide visitors one step at a time.
3. **Specificity over generality.** Real photos, real team members, real events beat stock imagery and generic mission statements.
4. **Warmth through restraint.** A warm palette, generous whitespace, and unhurried typography do more than gradients and animations.
5. **Accessible by design, not by afterthought.** WCAG AA as baseline; color choices and contrast matter from the first decision.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA compliance: minimum 4.5:1 contrast for body text, 3:1 for large text and UI components. Keyboard navigation throughout. Screen reader semantic HTML. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for any transitions or animations.
